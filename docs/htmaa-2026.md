# How To Make (almost) Anything 2026 (course site)

- URL: https://pub.cba.mit.edu/863.26/site/
- Verified: 2026-11-16 (index page and linked materials opened publicly, no login required unless noted)

Course site for the 2026 run of "MAS.863/4.140/6.9020 How To Make (almost) Anything", led by Neil Gershenfeld.

## What the index page itself shows (read directly)

- A weekly schedule from 09/09 to 12/15, 2026 (dates as labeled on the index). Weekly topics: introduction and computer-aided design, project management and computer-controlled cutting, embedded programming, 3D scanning and printing, electronics design, electronics production, computer-controlled machining, input devices, output devices, mechanical and machine design, wildcard week, networking and communications with interface and application programming, system integration, project development, and project presentation.
- Weekly recitation topics: version control and web development, parametric design, electronics, AI agents, EDA, debugging, CAM, Edge AI (the schedule lists slides and code links), motor and motion control, machine building, molding and casting, mesh networks.
- Site sections: Application, Schedule, People, Docs, Prior years, Search.

## Linked materials, opened and noted 2026-11-16

Each weekly topic on the index links out to class material, mostly on academy.cba.mit.edu. Everything below was opened on 2026-11-16; availability and contents are as observed that day. The lecture pages on academy.cba.mit.edu are dense link-and-keyword indexes rather than prose: each lists the concepts, tools, machines, and example "hello" boards for the week, ending with the group and individual assignments. Summaries below name the main coverage and the assignment; they are not exhaustive. The lecture pages themselves link onward to many example boards, code files, videos and datasheets; those second-level links were not opened, only noted as present.

### Week 09/09 - introduction + computer-aided design

- Introduction (principles and practices): https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/principles_practices/index.html - short framing page on digital fabrication with links to schedule, rights and responsibilities. Assignment: plan and sketch a potential final project.
- Computer-aided design: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/computer_design/index.html - broad survey of 2D (raster and vector) and 3D design, covering representations (BRep, FRep, VRep), solid modeling operations (extrude, revolve, loft, sweep, CSG, fillet/chamfer), parametric and algorithmic modeling, and interchange formats (STL, OBJ, STEP, glTF, ...). Long tool lists: FreeCAD, Fusion 360, Onshape, SolidWorks, Rhino/Grasshopper, Blender, OpenSCAD and many more, plus simulation (FEniCS, CalculiX, COMSOL, ...), audio/video tools, game engines, VR/AR, and AI tools (ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini, Midjourney/Stable Diffusion, text-to-CAD). Assignment: model a possible final project (raster, vector, 2D, 3D, render, animate, simulate), compress images/videos, and post it on your class page.

### Week 09/16 - project management + computer-controlled cutting

- Project management: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/project_management/index.html - version control history and Git (command line and GUIs, GitHub/GitLab/Gitea/Forgejo), web serving and development (HTML/CSS/JavaScript, Markdown, static site generators like Hugo and MkDocs, CI/CD), collaboration and videoconferencing tooling, and project management principles (80/20 and 95/5 rules, triage, serial vs parallel and spiral development, "document as you work", Mythical Man-Month). Assignment: sign the student agreement and commit it to your repo, work through a git tutorial, and build a personal site in the class archive describing you and your final project.
- Computer-controlled cutting: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/computer_cutting/index.html - vinyl cutters (Cricut, Cameo, Roland), laser cutters (Epilog, Trotec, xTool, ...), plasma, waterjet and hot-wire cutting. Applications (signs, pop-up cards, screen printing, flex circuits, antennas), laser physics basics (gain media, wavelengths), press-fit construction and joint types, living hinges, kerf and offset, safety (classes, venting, fires, material hazards like PVC), and settings (focus, power, speed, passes). Group assignment: safety training plus characterize your lab's lasercutter (focus, power, speed, kerf, joint clearance). Individual: cut something on the vinylcutter, and design, lasercut and document a parametric construction kit accounting for kerf (extra credit: multiple assembly modes, non-flat elements, engraving).

### Week 09/23 - embedded programming

- Embedded programming: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/embedded_programming/index.html - large reference page. Architectures (von Neumann vs Harvard, RISC vs CISC), memory types, peripherals (ADC, timers/PWM, USART, USB), and word sizes. Processor families used in class: AVR (ATtiny412/1614/3216, ATtiny1624, AVR128DB32), ARM SAMD (D11C/D11D/D21E/D51), STM32, Raspberry Pi RP2040/RP2350, ESP8266/ESP32 (incl. RISC-V ESP32-C3/C6). Languages and workflows: assembly, C/C++ with GCC/make, MicroPython/CircuitPython, Rust, Arduino ecosystem (cores, overhead, interrupts), plus AI coding assistants and embedded ML (TinyML, LiteRT, Edge Impulse). In-system programming in detail: SWD/JTAG with edbg/OpenOCD, UPDI with pyupdi/pymcuprog, classic AVR ISP with avrdude, bootloaders. Serial communication (RS232 timing, terminals, USB, FTDI), debugging approaches, simulation (Wokwi, AVR8js), and many example "hello" boards for each chip family with schematics and code. Group assignment: demonstrate and compare toolchains and workflows for available embedded architectures. Individual: browse a microcontroller datasheet, then write and test a program for an embedded system that interacts (input/output) and communicates (wired or wireless); extra credit for assembling the system and for trying different languages/environments.

### Week 09/30 - 3D scanning and printing

- 3D scanning and printing: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/scanning_printing/index.html - additive vs subtractive tradeoffs (complexity, access, waste; failure, resolution, time, cost). Materials (PLA, PETG, PHA, ABS/TPU/PEEK and composites; hygroscopic storage). Design rules with FreeCAD test files: overhang, clearance, unsupported angle, bridging, wall thickness, anisotropy/orientation, surface finish, infill. Printing processes and vendors: stereolithography (Formlabs), FDM (Prusa, Bambu, Ultimaker, RepRap heritage), DED/WAAM, SLS, binder jetting, PolyJet, bioprinting. File formats (STL dissected, PLY, AMF, 3MF, STEP), meshing and slicing software (MeshLab, PrusaSlicer, Cura), printing hosts (OctoPrint, Klipper). Scanning: photogrammetry (Meshroom, Polycam, KIRI Engine, COLMAP), structured light, laser, LIDAR, CT, and scanning practice notes (point clouds, watertight meshes, lighting/surface treatment). Group assignment: test the design rules for your printers. Individual: design and print an object that could not be made subtractively (small, few cm), and 3D scan an object (optionally print it).

### Week 10/07 - electronics design + first review

- Electronics design: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/electronics_design/index.html - component basics with governing equations (resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, LEDs, bipolar transistors and MOSFETs, regulators, op-amps, microcontrollers), circuit quantities (Kirchhoff's laws, power), and EDA tooling from Fritzing and EasyEDA through KiCad (with Fab's Fabrication Toolkit), Eagle/Fusion 360, and Altium; also HDLs (Verilog/VHDL) and PCB-as-code experiments. Simulation (Wokwi, Falstad, SPICE variants) and test equipment (supply, multimeter, oscilloscope, logic analyzer). Group assignment: use lab test equipment to observe an embedded microcontroller operating. Individual: use an EDA tool to design an embedded microcontroller system from the class inventory and run design-rule checks for fabrication (extra credit: simulate, try another workflow, design a case).
- First review: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/reviews/first.html - schedule a graded instructor review of your class site covering weekly assignments, final project section, and about-you page.

### Week 10/14 - electronics production

- Electronics production: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/electronics_production/index.html - PCB fabrication methods: chemical etching, machining (end mills and V-bits, fixturing, zeroing, climb vs conventional), vinyl-cutter flex circuits, laser approaches (UV, fiber, LIG), conductive ink printing. PCB materials (FR4, FR1, Kapton flex, copper weights), board houses (JLCPCB, PCBWay, OSH Park) and their design rules, layer stackups and via options. Assembly: solder types (lead-free, leaded, low-temp bismuth), technique (tinning, wetting, drag soldering, reflow with stencil/hot plate/hot air), rework and desoldering, debugging checklists (inspect joints, orientation, datasheets, supply voltages, signal probing), and CAM formats (Gerber/RS-274X, png workflows, FlatCAM, mods). Group assignment: characterize the design rules of your in-house PCB process and submit a design to a board house. Individual: make and test an embedded microcontroller system you designed (extra credit: another process).

### Week 10/21 - computer-controlled machining

- Computer-controlled machining: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/computer_machining/index.html - big-picture projects (OpenDesk, WikiHouse), machines from Shaper Origin and Maslow to ShopBot, Tormach and Haas. Stock materials (MDF, OSB, plywood, HDPE, aluminum composite), tooling (drills vs mills, flutes, coatings, up/down cut, ball end), speeds and feeds (chip load formula, cut depth and step-over rules of thumb), fixturing (vises, clamps, vacuum, sacrificial layers), toolpath strategies (conventional vs climb, roughing/finishing, adaptive clearing, trochoidal; 2 to 5 axis; tabs, onion skinning, dog-bones, nesting), CAM software (VCarvePro, Fusion 360, FreeCAD CAM, mods), and an extensive safety section. Group assignment: safety training plus test runout, alignment, fixturing, speeds, feeds, materials and toolpaths. Individual: make something big (meter-scale), design + mill + assemble (extra credit: no fasteners or glue, curved surfaces, three-axis toolpaths).

### Week 10/28 - input devices

- Input devices: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/input_devices/index.html - large survey with a working "hello" board example (schematic, board files, code, video) for each sensor type. Covers switches and debouncing, potentiometers, Hall effect and magnetic angle encoders (TLE493D ~10 bits, MT6835 ~21 bits), step-response capacitive sensing (self-capacitance, mutual/tx-rx, loading, remote sensing, caliper-style CVDT), temperature (NTC, RTD, thermocouple, IR), light (phototransistor, synchronous detection, RGB color with VEML6040), gesture/motion (Doppler radar RCWL-0516, pyroelectric HC-SR501), distance (VL53L0X/L1X/L5CX optical, HC-SR04 sonar, LIDAR), RTC, GPS (NMEA modules), IMUs from 3-axis ADXL343 to 9-axis BNO085/086 with fusion, microphones (I2S MEMS, analog electret), force/strain, and cameras (ESP32-CAM, XIAO Sense) with OpenCV. Group assignment: probe an input device's analog levels and digital signals. Individual: measure something - add a sensor to a board you designed and read it.

### Week 11/04 - output devices

- Output devices: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/output_devices/index.html - opens with electrical safety (current thresholds, polarity protection, level shifting). Power (supplies, batteries, LiPo charging, current measurement), then one or more example boards per output: LEDs (current limiting, PWM fading, Charlieplexing, NeoPixel/WS2812), high-power LED switching with MOSFETs (with thermal runaway and eye safety warnings), displays (HD44780 LCD, SSD1306 OLED, ILI9341 TFT, E Ink, composite video), audio (MP3 modules, I2S with MAX98357A, class-D PWM), DC motors with H-bridges (TB67H451), servos (50 Hz pulse control on several chips), brushless motors (BLDC theory, A4949 driver, ESCs), stepper motors (microstepping, DRV8428/DRV8421/DRV8251/DRV8263 drivers), solid state relays and AC wiring safety color codes, plus exotic actuators (shape memory, piezo, soft/pneumatic). Group assignment: measure the power consumption of an output device. Individual: add an output device to a board you designed and program it.

### Week 11/11 - mechanical + machine design

- Mechanical design: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/mechanical_design/index.html - vendor pointers (McMaster-Carr, Misumi), engineering principles (stress-strain, stiffness vs strength vs toughness, friction, backlash, force loops, elastic averaging, kinematic coupling, accuracy vs precision), materials and fasteners, drives (gears of many types, lead/ball screws, belts, capstan), guides and bearings, and a bestiary of mechanisms (flexures, series elastic, linkages, pantograph, deltabot, hexapod, CoreXY, Sarrus, Strandbeest). Group assignment (with machine design week): design a machine with mechanism + actuation + automation + function + user interface; build the mechanical parts and operate it manually.
- Machine design: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/machine_design/index.html - structural loops and error budgets, motion control (open/closed loop, PID to model predictive), timing and networking of machine modules, controllers and firmware (GRBL/grblHAL, Marlin, Duet, ODrive) and research platforms (Urumbu, Modular Things, Clank, Jubilee). Group assignment: actuate and automate the machine, document individual contributions, and demo it in the next class.

### Week 11/18 - wildcard week + second review

- Wildcard week: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/wildcard/index.html - open assignment: design and produce something with a digital process not covered elsewhere, documented for reproduction. Suggested areas: grinding, EDM, waterjet, plasma, welding, molding and casting, vacuum forming, folding/kirigami, robotics, soft/pneumatic actuation, pick and place, machine learning and vision, food printing, biotech (HTGAA, Bio Academy), textiles (knitting, embroidery with PEmbroider/Ink-Stitch, Fabricademy), composites.
- Second review: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/reviews/second.html - post a system diagram for the final project, list remaining tasks, make a schedule, and meet instructors for a graded review.

### Week 11/25 - networking and communications + interface and application programming

- Networking and communications: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/networking_communications/index.html - wired buses (UART/RS-232/485, serial broadcast and hop-count LED examples, I2C/TWI, SPI with naming conventions, SD cards, USB/TinyUSB, CAN, MODBUS, DMX), OSI layers, physical media and Shannon capacity, wireless ISM bands and FCC Part 15, protocols (Wi-Fi, ZigBee, Bluetooth/BLE, LoRa/LoRaWAN, optical, acoustic), modulation schemes (PCM through OFDM and UWB), channel sharing (ALOHA to CDMA/MIMO), error detection/correction, and Internet protocols (IPv4/6, DNS, DHCP, NAT, BGP, UDP/TCP/HTTP, sockets with example senders/receivers in JS/Python/C, WebSockets, Wireshark, SLIP). Radio chips with example boards: ESP32/ESP32-C3 and ESP8266 (web server/client, BLE, Web Bluetooth), MFRC522 RFID, nRF24L01+, RN4871/CC2541 Bluetooth, LoRa RA modules and Meshtastic, UWB positioning, software radio. Individual assignment: design, build and connect wired or wireless node(s) with network/bus addresses and local input/output. Group: send a message between two projects.
- Interface and application programming: https://academy.cba.mit.edu/classes/interface_application_programming/index.html - languages survey (C to Python/JS, low-code and dataflow like Node-RED and mods, AI coding tools with caveats), device interfaces (serial, I2C, USB, MQTT with a blink example), data interfaces (JSON, databases), user interfaces (ncurses, Tkinter, Qt, web frameworks), graphics (Canvas, SVG, WebGL/WebGPU with line/image/surface demos, Three.js, X Windows, game engines), audio/video, VR/AR (WebXR, A-Frame), math/plotting (NumPy, matplotlib, D3, Plotly), performance (Numba, Jax, threads, MPI, CUDA), machine learning (PyTorch/TensorFlow, ONNX, LLM APIs and MCP), deployment (REST, PWA, cloud, Docker/Kubernetes), and security. Individual assignment: write an application interfacing a user with an input/output device you made. Group: compare as many tool options as possible.

### Week 12/02 - system integration

- System integration: https://fabacademy.org/2026/classes/system_integration/index.html (hosted on fabacademy.org, loads publicly) - design philosophy (conceptual/preliminary/detailed, DFM, standard components, minimize parts), packaging (PCB mounting, wire routing, finish), testing (QA vs QC, shake/drop/burn-in, fuzzing), and a long failure-modes catalog: mechanical (stress concentration, loosening fasteners), wiring (strain relief, polarized connectors), components (MOSFET over-voltage, inductive flyback, reverse polarity), power (budgets, ground loops, bypass capacitors), software (memory leaks, race conditions, dependencies, scaling), plus manufacturing, repair and lifecycle (right to repair, disassembly, end of life). Assignment: design and document the system integration for your final project.

## Recitation materials

- Version control and Web development (9/10): https://pub.cba.mit.edu/863.26/site/recitations/version/index.html links two things, both opened: a Google Slides deck "Recitation 9/10/26" (https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Ae1F11cwjWdcyMdM3DU9LHsZwESRrRyfbNgYZnu6qGU, publicly readable; all slides reviewed visually) in four parts: Git basics (repos, staging, commits, commit messages, history), Forgejo hosting on CBA's server (account setup, SSH keys, cloning, no large files), making websites (HTML, publishing to the class site via rsync or Hugo Forgejo workflows, static generators), and an optional Git internals segment (what a commit contains, SHA-1 hashes, refs/HEAD/tags/branches, remotes, .gitignore, config hierarchy and aliases, stashing, amending, cherry-picking, rebasing and interactive rebasing, merge conflicts, the reflog, branch workflows and protecting main, worktrees, submodules, GPG signing); and a Git installation guide (https://pub.cba.mit.edu/dimitar.dimitrov/blog/tools/git/installing) with per-OS steps (Windows Git Bash or WSL2, macOS xcode-select, Ubuntu apt).
- Parametric design (9/17): https://pub.cba.mit.edu/alfonso.parra/HTMAA-CAD-Recitation/ - "Intro to CAD" recitation by Alfonso Parra Rubio, a full tutorial page. Design philosophy (design for the manufacturing process and its limits; parametric design saves rework), Fusion 360 student installation via Autodesk Education (with Onshape and FreeCAD as alternatives, Onshape for Linux), the "sketch on paper before modeling" advice with a cardboard puzzle example, sections on part design, assembly, export (STL/STP/DXF) and documentation (some sections marked "Text to come"), embedding a live Fusion model in a web page via iframe, and a curated resource list (MIT OpenCourseWare "How to CAD Almost Anything", Autodesk University, prior years' recitations).
- Electronics (9/24): Google Slides "HTMA Electronics" (https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1yi74tyA7pndkprdBvipFG7vPN_mt5oaKWDUpzZvqBeQ/edit?usp=sharing, publicly readable; all 50 slides reviewed visually) - a teach-the-board walkthrough: micro-USB connector, 3.3V regulator, why bypass capacitors matter (with ripple scope shots), resistor/LED basics and sizing a current-limiting resistor from forward voltage, pull-up/pull-down scenarios for button inputs, the ATSAMD21E, capacitive step-response buttons, MOSFETs as switches (N vs P channel, pull resistors and flyback diodes), Seeed XIAO RP2040/ESP32C3, and digital simulation in Wokwi vs Falstad with honest pros/cons, ending in assembly options on "QPad" boards from easy to "super challenge". Slide footers are dated 9/17/2024, so the deck is reused from a prior year.
- AI agents (10/01): https://pub.cba.mit.edu/863.26/site/recitations/AI/index.html - title page only (Eitan, Sayam, Ric); no slides or materials linked from it as of 2026-11-16.
- EDA (10/08): https://pub.cba.mit.edu/863.26/site/recitations/EDA/EDA_recitation_10-08.pdf - 28-page slide PDF "Electronic Design Automation or The art of untangling" (Quentin Bolsee, plus a breakout-boards section by Ben Weiss); all 28 pages reviewed visually. Good/bad board practice with board photos (strain on pads, mounting holes, labeling, integrating capacitive touch pads and flexures into the board), why breakout boards help repairability, then the EDA process: reading schematics (symbols, references, values, wire junction dots, labels, power symbols), board layout (ratsnest untangling, topology vs shape), iterating schematic and layout together, ending in a KiCad demo walkthrough with screenshots.
- Debugging (10/15): Google Slides "Debug anything" (https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1rCjAHXTtOT6wu7UAMmudnBsidMiCNB94gpR2nMy-xGw/edit?usp=sharing, publicly readable; all slides reviewed visually) - "How to debug almost anything (but mostly hardware)". Culture (ask for help, "nothing good happens after 2am", ego fries boards), the short list of usual culprits (milling issues/solder bridges, cold joints, component orientation, TX/RX swaps, power/ground shorts, missing power as the most common), spotting issues (backlighting, magnification, continuity testing caveats with big caps), board surgery techniques (magnet wire with strain relief, cutting traces, external caps/resistors), a suggested check order (power-to-ground resistance, voltages at known points, programming pin connectivity), multimeter functions (DC voltage, resistance, continuity) with photos, oscilloscope usage including UART decoding on a scope (with an ASCII table example) and an I2C scanner sketch, USB oscilloscopes/logic analyzers (Saleae, Analog Discovery) as portable test gear, and closing workflow tables of common hardware and software problems with first checks, plus in-circuit debugging notes (breakpoints in Arduino 2.X IDE, needs extra hardware).
- CAM (10/22): Google Slides "Using Fusion360 CAM" (https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1pmB81ebrsAB84UiewVSpeEZ-BaWs2pfRtmKS7dmI_tc/edit?usp=sharing, publicly readable; all 11 slides reviewed visually) - machining setup in Fusion 360 Manufacture: modeling stock, units, new setup, work coordinate system and origin choices (software must match hardware; Z zero on top vs bottom; "what went wrong" examples like cutting into the machine bed), triangular tabs vs square tabs, simulation, and a comparison note mapping the same concepts to Mastercam. (Some embedded images did not render in the PDF export used for review.)
- Edge AI (10/29): slides at https://pub.cba.mit.edu/863.26/site/recitations/edge_ai/ - "Embedded AI" by Erik Strand, an interactive Slidev deck of 46 slides, all slide bodies read 2026-11-16. Topic: keyword spotting on microcontrollers with a DS-CNN trained on Google Speech Commands v2 (1 s clips at 16 kHz; 85,511 training clips, 4,890 test clips), using MFCC features (49x10 frames) for streaming detection. The deck walks the network layers (dense, convolution, depthwise and pointwise, pooling, softmax), then gives a deep quantization treatment: integer and FP8 E4M3 formats, energy per operation, linear and symmetric quantization, quantize-dequantize error, requantizing to pure-integer M/2^n arithmetic, and post-training quantization vs quantization-aware training (straight-through estimator, learned step size). The results section reports (the deck's own measurements, not independently verified): training takes under an hour per variant on a laptop CPU; 17 variants are evaluated on three class boards (ESP32-S3, RP2040, RP2350); W8A8 post-training quantization holds accuracy (about 92% vs 91.6% for float32 on the full test set), aggressive low-bit PTQ collapses to 9-20% and QAT recovers the loss; on every board the C kernels reproduce the laptop integer reference exactly (0 mismatched clips across all 17 variants and 3 boards, float32 within 1e-4); no board makes the 100 ms hop budget; W8A8 is about 7x faster than float32 on the FPU-less RP2040; and float32 weights read from flash run at about half speed on the RP2350. One slide documents how AI was used in preparing the recitation itself (deck and code generated with an AI assistant, with a task table). The companion code link (https://forge.cba.mit.edu/erik.strand/embedded_ai) was not readable on 2026-11-16: automated access hit a "Making sure you're not a bot!" interstitial, and a direct browser visit returned a Forgejo 404 ("does not exist, has been removed or you are not authorized") - so the code was not read and its current public availability is unknown.
- Motor and motion control (11/05): https://pub.cba.mit.edu/863.26/site/recitations/motor_control/ - 34-slide deck by Quentin Bolsee (slide images; all 34 reviewed visually 2026-11-16, several slides include embedded animations). First principles: linear vs angular quantities, internal forces and centripetal acceleration, torque-force conversion, magnetic force and the basic DC motor. Motor types: brushed DC (brushes, commutator), H-bridge drive and PWM current control (slow/fast/mixed decay), brushless DC (active commutation, field-oriented control with Q/D axis feedback via Hall/encoder/sensorless), ESCs, steppers (200 steps/rev, microstepping tables), and hobby servos (DC motor + potentiometer + control loop under X-ray). Motion control: open vs closed loop, forward and inverse kinematics with CoreXY, CoreXYZ and DeltaXY examples, trapezoid velocity profiles and jerk, G-code's limits (line segments and arcs, no real-time feedback), continuous parametric paths as the "holy grail", typical controllers (RAMPS 1.4, GRBL), then controllerless architectures - Urumbu/Urumbot (stateless modules, one byte per microstep over USB) and Jake Read's "End of GCode" model-based control stack.
- Machine building (11/12), molding and casting (11/19), mesh networks (11/30), and the two help sessions (12/03, 12/10): no links on the index, nothing to open.

## Public discussion (Hacker News, 2026-11-16)

- Thread: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50514470 - "How to make almost anything", linking this exact course site, posted 2026-11-16 with about 150 points and roughly two dozen comments when read that day. Everything in this section is commenter opinion, not course fact.
- The main theme is a breadth-vs-depth debate. Critics call the weekly pages shallow enumerations of tools (one describes the CAD lecture as cramming dozens of tools into part of one lecture; another reports finding lists of buzzwords rather than prose). The description of the pages as link-and-keyword indexes matches the pages read above; whether that makes the teaching shallow is a commenter judgment. The lecture pages are dense link-and-keyword indexes with assignments, not prose tutorials. Defenders argue breadth-first is a deliberate pedagogical choice for a lab- and project-based course aimed at students from mixed backgrounds, and that an overview of what exists is valuable in itself.
- Other opinions in the thread: the pace is intense relative to the modest complexity of the actual projects; the format amounts to instructors setting goals while students learn on their own; one commenter calls it one of the best classes at the MIT Media Lab; a side thread swaps experiences using AI tools to generate 3D-printable designs; one asks how a self-studier should navigate the materials (whether to start with the recitations) - unanswered in the thread.

## Why it may be useful

The site is a broad public resource index and a starting point for self-study in digital fabrication: the weekly lecture pages are rich tool-and-concept indexes, and most recitation materials (slides, PDF, tutorial page) are publicly readable. Practical caveats: enrollment for 2026 is closed; the Edge AI code repo was not publicly readable at read time (bot interstitial for automated access, 404 in a direct browser visit); one linked page (exam timing) is dead; and several recitations (machine building, molding and casting, mesh networks, help sessions) have no linked materials on the index. Coverage here is the index's own links only: the lecture pages link onward to many more example boards, code files and videos that were not opened. No claim is made about content licensing.
