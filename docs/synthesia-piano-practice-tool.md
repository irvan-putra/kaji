# Synthesia: a falling-notes piano practice tool

Checked 2026-11-19. Source: the project's own About page, https://synthesiagame.com/About. Product claims below are the company's own words unless noted; pages can change.

## What the page says

- Synthesia is a game for learning piano using falling notes. A beginner can play songs immediately without reading sheet music, and can pick up traditional notation over time. For experienced players it positions itself as a sight-reading platform and a daily practice companion to piano lessons, with scoring and progression systems to track growth per piece.
- You can watch the notes fall and follow along, or connect a musical keyboard over USB or MIDI; Synthesia then listens to your playing.
- Songs use standard MIDI or MusicXML files. The page says they chose open, industry-standard formats so that every music editing app can load and export them, with "as few roadblocks between you and your favorite music as possible".
- A Music Store sells full MIDI arrangements, described as fully licensed by the original publisher for digital download, because finding high-quality MIDI songs is hard.
- Platforms listed: any PC or Mac from roughly the last 10 years (requires OpenGL 2.0, a 2004 standard), an iPad on iOS 9.3 or later, an Android tablet on Android 4.1 or later ("best experienced on a tablet-sized device"), and Chromebooks that can install Android apps (ChromeOS 65 or later for USB MIDI).
- The page carries one diagram of how it works: https://cdn.synthesia.app/images/faqHowItWorks.png (linked, not copied; reuse rights not checked).

## Project history (from the same page)

- 2006: introduced as "Piano Hero"; no musical-keyboard support yet; site runs on donations.
- 2007: Activision asks for a name change to avoid confusion with Guitar Hero. A name contest picks "Synthesia"; the Mac version ships with the new name.
- 2008: the Learning Pack, a paid upgrade with educational features, replaces the donation model.
- 2010: featured on UK television (Strictly Come Dancing).
- 2011: the page claims Synthesia became the most popular way to produce piano tutorial videos online (company claim, not independently verified).
- 2012: educator features; iPad debut.
- 2013: six display languages added; Music Store launches.
- 2014: Android tablet release.
- 2015: how-to guides and usability fixes.
- 2017: Windows 10 Bluetooth MIDI and support for key lights on The ONE Smart Keyboard.
- 2018: Chromebook support.
- 2021: multi-track song recording added.

## Ideas to borrow (our reading, not Synthesia's claims)

- Build on open file formats (MIDI, MusicXML) so users are not locked into one editor; relevant to our own MIDI work.
- Falling notes as a beginner interface: play first, notation later, with scoring to keep practice measurable.
- The funding path is documented in public: donations, then a paid feature pack, then a licensed content store. A small project can start free and add paid layers without changing the core.
- Naming: "Piano Hero" survived about a year before a Guitar Hero proximity complaint forced a rename. Picking a name far from big brands from the start is cheaper.
