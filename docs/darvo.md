# DARVO: deny, attack, reverse victim and offender, explained

Checked 25 November 2026. Public sources only.

DARVO is an acronym for "Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender": a reaction a person who did harm may show when they are held accountable for it. Psychologist Jennifer Freyd coined the term in 1997.

| Step | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Deny | The person denies the harm ever took place. |
| Attack | Confronted with evidence, they attack the person who was harmed, or that person's family and friends. |
| Reverse Victim and Offender | They claim to be the real victim, reversing the roles, often including blaming the actual victim. |

## What the sources say

- The term and the theory come from psychologist Jennifer Freyd (1997).
- The pattern is common: in one study of undergraduates who had confronted someone over a harmful event, 72% of the confronted people used DARVO, across offenses from betrayed secrets to interpersonal violence.
- It appears beyond interpersonal violence: the Wikipedia article cites cases in medical malpractice, workplace bullying and toxic workplace culture, and institutional responses, where reporting systems can be turned against the person reporting.
- Knowing the pattern blunts it: in one experiment, observers who had learned how DARVO works were less likely to blame the victim and more likely to hold the perpetrator responsible.

## Where Sanggup's guidance comes from

The counter Sanggup teaches (stay with the specific behavior and the rule or facts, and do not get pulled into defending yourself) is our own teaching judgment, consistent with the awareness finding above, not a prescription quoted from Freyd.
