Talk reading, 22 Nov 2026. Session: (Human) Attention is All You Need, IDSW 2026 day two, 10:55-11:25 on 22 Nov, listed speaker Zain Fathoni (BookThatApp) on the official agenda. The attribution here is stronger than agenda-matching alone: the slide footers carry the talk title, the closing slide is signed, and the talk has its own page at zainf.dev/idsw-2026. This note is based on photographed slides shared with us while the talk ran; the photos are not reproduced here. References were checked on 22 Nov 2026 against the linked originals.

The talk's arc: two psychology classics about how every reader fills gaps you did not write; Kahneman's WYSIATI applied to working with coding agents; a working loop of Notice, Choose, Delegate, Review, Enough; a delegation boundary, execution to the agent, judgment questions kept with you; then an inward turn about noticing strain without turning it into a verdict; a demo of the speaker's own recorded-time tool, Cukup; and the closing line: let AI help you do the work, keep the decision to say cukup (enough). The title is an allusion to the Transformer paper, which the talk's own references list marks as a title reference only.

The argument: choose before you delegate

The middle of the talk turned the psychology into a working rule. A better agent setup cannot choose for you: picking the P0 is still your job, and the question that matters is what evidence would change your mind. The closing loop put the pieces together: Notice what is hard and still open, Choose one priority and what "enough" means, Delegate execution and exploration to the agent, Review the evidence against "enough", then decide it is Enough (stop or continue, and write what remains). "Enough" carried three senses: one priority chosen, the evidence the work needs, and the moment you personally stop and recheck. Delegating ten tasks you cannot review, the talk argued, is not leverage; it is ten unreviewed results.

The closing diagrams drew the delegation boundary. On the agent's side: the diff, the new package, the schema change, the refactor plan, the docs update. On your side, the judgment questions: correct? why is it failing? edge cases? safe? secure? reversible? who owns the result? A related slide shown in the same session (DevFest-branded; its speaker attribution is unresolved, so no name is attached here) made the Review step concrete with an illustrative example: one pull request of 319 files and 16,397 added lines, reviewed in a single session, surfaced one finding after the context folded four times, while the same change split into five behavioral slices (accounting, authentication, checkout contract, settings and lifecycle, plus a skeptic pass whose rule was "no failing test, no finding") surfaced five findings and eight failing tests. The slide's rule: split by behavior, not by file count, because the worst bugs cross file boundaries. Treat those numbers as that slide's example, not a benchmark.

The inward turn

The last segment turned the same lens on the person doing the delegating. The speaker showed his own recorded working-time dashboard, long weeks included, with a warning label he called over-identification: "I'm working late again. I'm failing." His alternative, drawn from Neff's three elements, was plain observation: see what is hard right now, without ignoring it or making it bigger, and review the record before judging. He demonstrated the review-by-video practice with his own recording (a 16-minute recording, 22 chapters, 12 seconds to find a moment: figures specific to that demo). Common humanity came next: "everyone else keeps up, only I can't" is isolation talking, and asking for review or cover is normal engineering practice, not weakness. Self-kindness closed the arc with responsibility kept: when a risky change cannot yet be explained, the answer is not "catch up tonight, just work harder" but a triage you can say out loud to the team: I handle this today, this can wait, or this needs help to review. A joke about letting even the conference wait for a newly released game made the same point; it was a stage joke, not a release announcement. The segment's rule for open work: if you have not checked it, it is not done, so write down what is open, who needs to know, and the next step.

He also mentioned a community he leads, Vibe From Cafe, as his own example of navigating the AI shift together; that is his project and his claim, cited here as such, not an endorsement.

The closing

The closing slide put the thesis in one line: let AI help you do the work, and keep the decision to say cukup, Indonesian for "enough", which is also the name of his tool. The references slide and a signed Q&A slide closed the talk, pointing at zainf.dev/idsw-2026 and cukup.dev.

Where the practice version lives

The exercises derived from this talk, pinning meaning down for two readers, choosing before delegating with an explicit "enough", splitting large reviews by behavior, and noticing strain without a verdict, are stages cd21-cd23 in the Coding path of Sanggup, written as paper exercises with the same attributions.
