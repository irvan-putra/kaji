Tokenomics of Testing: an IDSW 2026 talk, checked

Talk reading, 22 Nov 2026. Three slides photographed during an IDSW 2026 day-two session; the talk is longer than the photographed slides, so this is a partial reading, not a whole-talk summary. The slides are in Indonesian; quotes are our translations. The deck is public: Tokenomics of Testing (Bahasa Indonesia) at the speaker's own domain (a JS slide app, so we confirmed the title only; slide text below comes from the photos). Attribution: the slide byline Ivo (@ivokun) and the deck hosted at ivokun.com; we did not verify identity beyond that, and the original photos are not reproduced.

What the slides say

  • "Dua angka" (Two numbers). Two X posts are contrasted. In one (Samuel Robertson, 10 Nov 2026), an agent asked to remove something reportedly added 4,000 lines of tests asserting the thing no longer exists, "so that it can't come back without us knowing". In the other (Peter Steinberger, 8 Oct 2026), the project owner reports a running count of about 1.3 million deleted weak tests and CI running twice as fast.
  • "Tanpa bukti, jangan hapus." (Without evidence, don't delete.) A four-question checklist for any test: What behavior does it protect? What regression would make it fail? What evidence already exists? Does it exist only for tests? Beside it, a screenshot of an X post (28 Sep 2026) the slide captions "sampah" (trash), quoting an agent commit message that claims 665 tests and 43,871 lines removed and 148 tests kept, and says it would run the remaining tests once to confirm they pass.
  • Closing: "Bukan lebih sedikit test. Test yang benar, dibayar dengan sadar." (Not fewer tests. The right tests, paid for deliberately.) Three lenses: measure the suite in tokens, not just test count; one piece of evidence before deleting, one before writing; budget per read, not per project.

The surrounding discourse is real: several Sep-Nov 2026 guides describe agents deleting or weakening tests to get green runs, for example this guide and this one. That pattern existing does not turn any single anecdote into a measured result.

What we keep

The talk's own thesis is the safe frame: the two numbers are not a "more tests versus fewer tests" rule. The transferable practice is evidence-first test review: before writing or deleting a test, name the behavior it protects, the regression that would fail it, and why existing coverage does not already catch it. Token cost per suite read is a budgeting lens, not a quality metric.

Sanggup carries the practice version: the Coding path stages "Review tests with evidence first" and "Label a claim before you repeat it" turn the checklist and the source-strength distinction into written exercises.
