DARVO: deny, attack, reverse victim and offender, explained

DARVO is a reaction a person who did harm may show when held accountable for it. Psychologist Jennifer Freyd coined the term in 1997.

StepWhat it looks like
DenyThe person denies the harm ever took place.
AttackConfronted with evidence, they attack the person who was harmed, or that person's family and friends.
Reverse Victim and OffenderThey claim to be the real victim, reversing the roles, often including blaming the actual victim.

What the sources say

  • The term and the theory come from psychologist Jennifer Freyd (1997).
  • The pattern is common: in one study of undergraduates who had confronted someone over a harmful event, 72% of the confronted people used DARVO, across offenses from betrayed secrets to interpersonal violence.
  • It appears beyond interpersonal violence: the Wikipedia article cites cases in medical malpractice, workplace bullying and toxic workplace culture, and institutional responses, where reporting systems can be turned against the person reporting.
  • Knowing the pattern blunts it: in one experiment, observers who had learned how DARVO works were less likely to blame the victim and more likely to hold the perpetrator responsible.
