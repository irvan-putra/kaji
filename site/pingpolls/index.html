PingPolls: a conversation, a choice and a timing signal

PingPolls combines one-question-at-a-time forms, conditional routing, AI-assisted drafting and analysis, and card-based preference questions. The interesting method is the separation between what people choose and a timing-derived score. That score needs care.

Timing is not truth. The vendor's own whitepaper says Certiscore is a heuristic, not a bot detector, and its constants have not been validated against labelled confidence data. Slow can mean thoughtful, interrupted or using assistive technology. Fast can mean careless.

Three different layers

Conversational presentation

Ask one question at a time and route the next question from earlier answers. This can reduce the amount to read at once, but a friendly chat can still contain leading questions. Keep consent, context, skip/exit routes and correction available. Public pages describe this flow; no authenticated builder or real survey was tested here.

Pairwise preference

Show two options and ask which is preferred, rather than asking people to rate every option highly. PingPolls describes two to ten options, adaptive pair ordering and skipping comparisons implied by earlier picks. This is not the same as a complete fixed set of pairs or a MaxDiff experiment.

Ordering depends on earlier respondents, so question-order effects remain. Preferences can cycle: A beats B, B beats C, C beats A. The whitepaper says cyclic options not placed by its topological sort are appended at the end, an algorithmic choice rather than proof of a strict preference order.

Sentiment cards

One card receives a two-sided verdict such as Yes/No, up to twenty cards. This is a chosen response, distinct from the product's separate AI emotion analysis of open text. Certiscore applies to preference-ranking and sentiment-card choices, not every question type.

AI analysis

The vendor describes embedding-based relevance signals, nearest-description emotion matching, and retrieval/counting/summaries in Ask AI. These are not truth tests or psychological assessments. Its whitepaper discloses unvalidated thresholds, false-positive fixes, token limits and no published summary-quality evaluation.

How the timing score is described

  1. Measure browser-side time after a card becomes selectable. The documentation says the entrance animation's first 150 ms are excluded.
  2. Use that respondent's twentieth-percentile choice time on the same question as the reference.
  3. Apply an exponential decay to delay above that reference: exp(-1.5 × excess_ms /10000). The rate and reference are design choices.
  4. For sentiment cards, the chosen side receives at least 501 of 1000. For preference ranking, timing narrows score gaps without changing the order, then the top option is scaled to 1000.

Worked check: a choice 2500 ms above reference has certainty about 0.687, not a 68.7% probability of honesty. A choice at reference gets 1. With only two options there is only one pick; it is its own reference, so timing has no effect. A score near 1000 is a relative constructed index, not a population percentage or calibrated confidence interval.

What can go wrong

  • Notifications, device speed, difficult images and accessibility needs can alter delay. Timing cannot identify the reason.
  • Times come from the respondent's browser; the vendor says its server cannot prove they are real. The score is not proof of a human respondent.
  • Adaptive pair order and the message that reaction speed counts can influence behavior.
  • The whitepaper says CSV exports contain scores, not raw choice times or timestamps. That limits independent timing reconstruction from that export.
  • A sentiment percentage derived from weighted scores is not necessarily the percentage who selected Yes. Preserve raw choices and explain weighted results separately.
  • None of this fixes a biased sample, a loaded question or missing groups.

Privacy and review

The whitepaper says AI analysis must be enabled per question. Answer text, question text and form title go to an embedding provider. Ask AI has masking and truncation, according to the vendor, but that is not a reason to upload personal respondent data without consent and a data-processing review. Read the privacy policy, retention and export limits before collecting real answers.

What to borrow for learning

Start with a decision, write neutral questions, pilot with the intended audience, compare options carefully and report who answered. Use a conversational flow when it helps the respondent, not to hide uncertainty. If timing is explored, make it an optional research signal, keep accessibility routes and raw choices, and do not judge a person by speed.

Sanggup's Surveying people path now has independently written practice on conversational questions, pairwise choices and timing limits. It is a learning path, not a PingPolls integration: no account link, real survey collector, timer or AI call is added.
